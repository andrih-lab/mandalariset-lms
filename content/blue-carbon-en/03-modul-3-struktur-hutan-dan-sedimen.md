# Module 3. Forest structure and sediment

## Module overview

This module explains the four mangrove forest types, why mangrove species grow in bands from sea to land, how mangrove forests differ from other forests, and the role of mangroves in trapping sediment, then uses all of it to define the three strata of the Sample Forest (A, B, and C). The zonation map and the reasons for the strata produced here become the basis for the plot-number calculation in Module 6 and all carbon stock calculations after it.

## Learning objectives

After completing this module, participants are able to:

1. Name the four mangrove forest types (fringe, riverine or estuarine, basin, dwarf) and describe where each is located.
2. Explain the three factors that shape mangrove species zonation (duration of flooding, salinity, competition between species) and why zonation must not be assumed to be the same on every coast.
3. Name two features of mangrove forest that distinguish it from rainforest (little understory and litter) and the consequence for the carbon measurement plan.
4. Assess the statement that mangroves "build land" using the facts about sediment trapping and the caveats from Hogarth.
5. Calculate stratum area (hectares and percent) from shoreline length and band width, then draw a simple zonation map of the Sample Forest.
6. Write the reason for defining each stratum and connect it to the plot requirement per stratum in Module 6.

## Content

### 3.1 Forest types, zonation, and strata

**Four mangrove forest types.** KD (chapter 1) and the guidebook distinguish four types based on location and stand form:

| Type | Location | Features |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fringe | Along the shoreline | Flooded at almost every tide |
| Riverine or estuarine | Along rivers and estuaries | Influenced by river flow and its sediment |
| Basin | Behind the two types above | Rarely flooded |
| Dwarf or scrub | Varies | A few metres tall, even less than 1 m |

Aboveground biomass differs enormously between types. Fringe and riverine forests in the Indo-Pacific can exceed 500 Mg/ha, whereas dwarf mangrove has only about 8 Mg/ha (KD chapter 1). Hogarth (H 4.1) adds that the three landscape settings (deltas, macrotidal estuaries, basins behind them) are not sharply separated, so a single site often has features of all three.

**Zonation.** On many coasts, mangrove species are arranged in bands parallel to the shoreline and each band is dominated by one species. Four explanations have been proposed (H 4.1.1): propagules are sorted by the tide; physical conditions change from sea to land; succession and competition between species; changing landscape. Research in Darwin Harbour, Australia, shows that the duration and frequency of flooding matter more than salinity. Other experiments show that most species grow best at low salinity, and species that tolerate a wide salinity range grow slowly and so lose out in competition at good sites. Zonation is therefore the combined result of physical conditions and competition. Hogarth concludes that the only general rule of mangrove zonation is that there is no generally applicable rule.

The practical consequence: the order of species from sea to land in the Sample Forest (illustration) must not be copied to another site. Stratum definition must be based on observation at that site itself.

**From zonation to strata.** A stratum is a part of a site whose carbon stock is relatively uniform (Module 6). Zonation helps because bands that differ in species and age usually differ in biomass and soil. The bases used to define strata, according to the guidebook and KD, are species and density of vegetation, distance from the sea edge, soil properties and landform, and land use. KD recommends placing plots at several distances from the sea edge because soil depth and species composition change from sea to land (KD chapter 1; BC chapter 2).

### Sample Forest data: zonation map and strata

The Sample Forest (illustrative data, fictitious site) of 564 ha is a strip along the coast. In this sketch the shoreline length is 6,000 m and the band width is measured perpendicular to the coast from sea to land. The following sketch is read from the top (sea) to the bottom (land):

```
SEA
==========================================================
STRATUM A  pioneer seaward mangrove         width 300 m
           Sonneratia, Avicennia            180 ha
----------------------------------------------------------
STRATUM B  mature Rhizophora forest         width 440 m
           Rhizophora                       264 ha
----------------------------------------------------------
STRATUM C  landward mangrove                width 200 m
           Bruguiera, Ceriops               120 ha
==========================================================
LAND          (shoreline length 6,000 m, sketch not to scale)
```

| Stratum | Dominant vegetation | Band width (m) | Length (m) | Area (ha) | Share of site (%) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | Sonneratia, Avicennia (pioneer seaward) | 300 | 6,000 | 180 | 31.9 |
| B | Mature Rhizophora | 440 | 6,000 | 264 | 46.8 |
| C | Bruguiera, Ceriops (landward mangrove) | 200 | 6,000 | 120 | 21.3 |
| Total |  | 940 |  | 564 | 100 |

Reasons for defining the strata, with the answers written each time on the "Zonation" sheet:

| Stratum | Reason for definition | Stock evidence (illustration, Mg C/ha) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | At the sea edge and flooded most often; pioneer species; younger and lower stand | Live trees 70, soil 380, total 483 |
| B | Mature Rhizophora, the largest and densest stand | Live trees 120, soil 495, total 670 |
| C | Behind the Rhizophora forest, rarely flooded; species change to Bruguiera and Ceriops | Live trees 95, soil 430, total 565 |

Mean stocks differ considerably between strata (483, 670, and 565 Mg C/ha). That is why the site is calculated per stratum and then summed by area, not averaged from one plot to the whole site. The site total by area is 180 × 483 + 264 × 670 + 120 × 565 = 331,620 Mg C, or a weighted mean of 588 Mg C/ha (complete data per pool are in the Guide tab).

**Worked example (illustration): stratum area from band width.** How many hectares is stratum A?

1. Area in m² = length × width = 6,000 × 300 = 1,800,000 m².
2. 1 ha = 10,000 m², so area = 1,800,000 ÷ 10,000 = 180 ha.
3. Its share of the site = 180 ÷ 564 × 100 = 31.9%.

**Relation to Module 6.** The number of plots is calculated per stratum with n = (t × s ÷ E)², so a stratum with small variability needs fewer plots. Example in Module 6: a mean of 400 Mg C/ha with a standard deviation of 120 needs 36 plots (10% reserve makes it 40), whereas a standard deviation of 60 needs only 9 plots. Strata A, B, and C defined here are those calculation units, and the standard deviation of each stratum from a pilot survey will determine its number of plots. Strata made without a field basis do not reduce variability, so they do not reduce the number of plots.

### 3.2 Mangrove forest compared with other forests, and sediment

**Difference from rainforest.** Mangrove forest is poor in species and simple in structure (H 4.1.2; KD chapter 1). Two features matter for carbon inventory:

- The understory is almost absent; on the forest floor there are generally only mangrove seedlings.
- The litter layer is almost absent, because fallen leaves are quickly eaten by crabs or carried away by the tide.

So understory and litter store only small amounts of carbon and often do not need to be measured separately. In the Sample Forest, litter is combined with dead trees and downed dead wood into one pool (8 to 15 Mg C/ha per stratum), far smaller than soil (380 to 495 Mg C/ha).

**Worked example (illustration): the effect of one plot in tall forest on a site of mixed type.** A 100 ha site consists of 80 ha of tall forest (aboveground biomass 500 Mg/ha) and 20 ha of dwarf mangrove (8 Mg/ha).

1. The true site value = 80 × 500 + 20 × 8 = 40,000 + 160 = 40,160 Mg, or 401.6 Mg/ha.
2. If all plots are placed in the tall forest, the estimate = 100 × 500 = 50,000 Mg.
3. Overestimate = (50,000 − 40,160) ÷ 40,160 × 100 = 24.5%.

This error disappears if tall and dwarf forest are made two strata. The numbers 500 and 8 Mg/ha come from KD chapter 1; the areas of 80 and 20 ha are illustrations.

**Sediment trapping.** Mangroves trap sediment (H 4.1.3). Current in tidal channels is usually more than 1 m/second, and once it enters the forest it falls below 0.1 m/second because it is impeded by trunks, prop roots, and pneumatophores. Mud settles when the water is still, and the ebb current is too weak to lift it again. Roughly 80% of the sediment carried in from coastal waters is trapped inside the forest. The soil surface rises by up to 11 mm per year at the highest rate ever measured, although most are lower, and the rate is proportional to the density of pneumatophores. Leaves, twigs, dead wood, and root growth in the soil also raise the surface.

**Worked example (illustration): sediment trapped and surface rise.** A forest receives 50 t of sediment per hectare per year from the tide.

1. Sediment trapped = 50 × 0.80 = 40 t/ha/year.
2. At the highest rate ever measured (11 mm/year), the rise over 30 years = 11 × 30 = 330 mm.
3. At 3 mm/year (an invented number for a more common rate), the rise over 30 years = 3 × 30 = 90 mm.

**Do mangroves build new land?** Hogarth (H 4.1.3) cautions against overstating this. On many coasts, mudflats form in front of the forest without help from mangroves. Elsewhere, mangrove trees fall because the shore is eroding. A more accurate statement: mangroves follow a coast that is growing and retreat from a coast that is eroding, and they trap sediment so the soil surface rises. Do not write in a report that mangroves "made" land without accretion data or soil surface measurements at that site.

**Summary.**

- Four forest types: fringe, riverine, basin, dwarf; biomass ranges from 8 to more than 500 Mg/ha.
- Zonation is the combined result of flooding duration, salinity, and competition; there is no general rule.
- Strata are defined from field observation, then tested with pilot plots (Module 6).
- Understory and litter are small; about 80% of the sediment that enters is trapped in the forest.

## Self-study exercises

1. **Stratum area.** In the Sample Forest, stratum C is a band 6,000 m long and 200 m wide. Calculate its area in hectares and its share of the 564 ha site.
2. **Mixed site.** A 250 ha site consists of 200 ha of tall forest (400 Mg/ha aboveground biomass) and 50 ha of dwarf mangrove (8 Mg/ha); all numbers are illustrations. Calculate the correct total biomass and the overestimate if the whole site is treated as tall forest.
3. **Sample Forest: weighted total.** With totals per hectare A 483, B 670, and C 565 Mg C/ha and areas 180, 264, and 120 ha, calculate the site total and the weighted mean. Compare with the simple mean of the three strata.
4. **Sample Forest: the effect of measuring only stratum B.** If the entire 564 ha site is assumed to have a stock of 670 Mg C/ha, what is the overestimate relative to the total in problem 3, in Mg C and percent?
5. **Assessing a claim.** A report writes: "Because mangroves trap 80% of sediment, the mangroves at this site are proven to have built 5 ha of new land over 10 years." Write two reasons the claim is not yet supported, and what data are needed.

**Answer key**

1. Area = 6,000 × 200 = 1,200,000 m²; ÷ 10,000 = 120 ha. Share = 120 ÷ 564 × 100 = 21.3%.
2. Correct: 200 × 400 + 50 × 8 = 80,000 + 400 = 80,400 Mg. Mistaken estimate: 250 × 400 = 100,000 Mg. Overestimate = (100,000 − 80,400) ÷ 80,400 × 100 = 24.4%.
3. Total: 86,940 + 176,880 + 67,800 = 331,620 Mg C. Weighted mean = 331,620 ÷ 564 = 588 Mg C/ha. Simple mean = (483 + 670 + 565) ÷ 3 = 572.7 Mg C/ha, lower by 15.3 Mg C/ha because stratum B, the largest and richest, is given the same weight as the others.
4. Estimate = 564 × 670 = 377,880 Mg C. Difference = 377,880 − 331,620 = 46,260 Mg C; 46,260 ÷ 331,620 × 100 = 13.9% too high.
5. First, trapping 80% of sediment shows that sediment is retained within the forest, not that the shoreline advanced; a coast can grow for other reasons or erode (H 4.1.3). Second, the figure of 5 ha is not accompanied by measurement. Data needed: imagery or shoreline maps from two dates and measurements of accretion or soil surface height at the site.

## Find the error

**Case A (illustration).** A participant writes on the "Zonation" sheet: "Stratum A is a band 6,000 m × 300 m = 1,800,000 m². Divided by 100,000 that equals 18 ha. Stratum A is only 3.2% of the 564 ha site."

**Case B (illustration).** A Sample Forest summary writes: "Plots were placed only in stratum B because it is the easiest to reach. A mean of 670 Mg C/ha multiplied by 564 ha gives 377,880 Mg C for the whole Sample Forest."

**Key**

- Case A. The error is in the unit conversion: 1 ha = 10,000 m², not 100,000 m². Correct figure: 1,800,000 ÷ 10,000 = 180 ha, or 31.9% of the site. How to detect it: compare with the total. If 18 + 264 + 120 = 402 ha, it does not equal 564 ha. The sum of stratum areas must equal the site area.
- Case B. The error is using the mean of one stratum for the whole site. The correct total is 331,620 Mg C (weighted mean 588 Mg C/ha). That estimate is 46,260 Mg C (13.9%) too high. How to detect it: check whether every stratum has plots, and whether the site total was calculated from the sum of area × mean per stratum.

## The role of AI and example prompts

AI is used to draft spreadsheet formulas, explain terms, and check calculations. AI does not replace field observation: stratum definition remains based on site data and the books.

**Prompt 1: drafting formulas.** "I have a table in Excel: column B holds shoreline length (m), column C band width (m) for three strata in rows 2 to 4. Write formulas for area in hectares and percent of the total, including function names for Excel on Mac." Check: calculate one row with a calculator (6,000 × 300 ÷ 10,000 = 180 ha), make sure the sum of stratum areas equals 564 ha, and make sure the percentages sum to 100.

**Prompt 2: explaining terms.** "Explain the difference between fringe, riverine, basin, and dwarf mangroves in simple language for beginner participants, with one sentence on the location of each." Check: match the four types and their locations with the table in section 3.1 (KD chapter 1). If the AI adds types or numbers, mark them and look them up in the book.

**Prompt 3: checking a calculation.** "Check my calculation: stratum B 440 m × 6,000 m = 2,640,000 m² = 264 ha, 46.8% of 564 ha. Show each step." Check: repeat the calculation yourself and compare with the stratum area table.

**Example of an AI answer that can be wrong.** Suppose the AI answers: "Mangrove zonation is determined mainly by salinity: Avicennia in the saltiest zone, then Rhizophora, then Bruguiera, and this order applies on every coast, so strata can be defined from the order of species alone." This answer is wrong for three reasons. First, research in Darwin Harbour shows the duration and frequency of flooding matter more than salinity. Second, most species grow best at low salinity. Third, Hogarth concludes that there is no generally applicable zonation rule (H 4.1.1). How to catch it: look in H 4.1.1 for the keywords flooding, salinity, and general rule; a statement that the order of species applies on every coast must be checked against the book before use.

## Excel notes: Mac and Windows

In Excel or Google Sheets in Indonesian, function names differ and the argument separator is a semicolon. For example SUM becomes JUMLAH, AVERAGE becomes RATA.RATA (RATA2 in older versions), SUMPRODUCT becomes SUMPRODUK, ROUND becomes BULATKAN. The table below uses English names and comma separators. Locking a cell address uses a dollar sign before the column letter and row number, for example `$D$5`.

| Step | Mac | Windows | Google Sheets |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Area in ha from m² (cell B2 length, C2 width) | `=B2*C2/10000` | `=B2*C2/10000` | `=B2*C2/10000` |
| Sum of stratum areas | `=SUM(D2:D4)` | `=SUM(D2:D4)` | `=SUM(D2:D4)` |
| Percent of total (total in D5) | `=D2/$D$5*100`, then format to 1 decimal | same | same |
| Lock the total cell address | Cmd+T with the cursor on the cell address, or type the dollar sign | F4 with the cursor on the cell address, or type the dollar sign | Type the dollar sign directly |
| Weighted site total | `=SUMPRODUCT(D2:D4,G2:G4)` (area × stock per ha) | same | same |
| Weighted mean | `=SUMPRODUCT(D2:D4,G2:G4)/SUM(D2:D4)` | same | same |
| Round to 1 decimal | `=ROUND(H2,1)` | `=ROUND(H2,1)` | `=ROUND(H2,1)` |
| Number format | Cmd+1, Number tab | Ctrl+1, Number tab | Format > Number menu |
